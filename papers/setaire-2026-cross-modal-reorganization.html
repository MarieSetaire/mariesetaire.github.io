Cross-Modal Reorganization of Auditory Cortex in Deafness. Adaptive or Maladaptive Mechanism?

Marie Setaire

Journal of Artificial Intelligence General Science (JAIGS), vol. 9, no. 1, pp. 145–154. Published 5 June 2026.

Download PDF

Abstract

Cross-modal reorganization refers to the process by which auditory brain regions begin handling non-auditory — mainly visual — information after peripheral hearing loss. Traditionally, this has been seen as an adaptive form of brain plasticity. Growing evidence, however, reveals a more complex picture. In people with normal hearing, audiovisual predictive mechanisms help facilitate speech perception. When hearing is acutely degraded by noise vocoding, the temporal voice areas (TVA) maintain voice-selectivity by drawing on preserved temporal cues. But with prolonged deafness, the TVA undergo structural cross-modal reconfiguration, which produces a lasting visual bias in voice processing that persists even after successful cochlear implant (CI) rehabilitation. This review follows the continuum from normal predictive coding, through the effects of acute degradation, to long-term TVA reorganization. It examines what drives the shift from adaptive to maladaptive reorganization and considers how neuroimaging might one day be used to monitor this process in clinical settings.

Keywords: cross-modal reorganization; temporal voice areas; cochlear implant; predictive coding; vocoding; deafness; brain plasticity; fNIRS; aural preference; audiovisual integration
